Prevention library

The seven conditions FieldScope's diagnosis tool is tuned to recognise, with the symptoms and preventive steps for each.

No signs of disease

Low concern

What to look for

  • Even green colour across the leaf
  • No dead or sunken tissue
  • Low variation in surface tone

Keep it that way

  • Keep up your current watering and feeding routine
  • Check again after the next few days of weather change
  • Scout weekly during humid stretches, when disease spreads fastest

Early blight (Alternaria)

Moderate concern

What to look for

  • Dark brown, target-like spots
  • Yellowing tissue surrounding lesions
  • Spots concentrated toward leaf edges

Prevent and treat

  • Remove and destroy affected lower leaves
  • Improve airflow by spacing or pruning nearby foliage
  • Apply a copper-based or chlorothalonil fungicide per label
  • Avoid overhead watering — water at the base instead

Late blight (Phytophthora)

High concern

What to look for

  • Dark green-black, water-soaked patches
  • Pale fuzzy growth may appear on the underside
  • Rapid spread across neighbouring leaves

Prevent and treat

  • Isolate or remove infected plants immediately
  • Apply a protectant fungicide before the next rain
  • Avoid working in the field while foliage is wet
  • Alert neighbouring growers — this spreads fast between fields

Powdery mildew

Moderate concern

What to look for

  • White or grey powdery coating
  • Leaf curling or slight distortion
  • Coating that rubs off easily

Prevent and treat

  • Prune to open up airflow around affected plants
  • Apply a sulfur or potassium bicarbonate spray
  • Avoid high-nitrogen feeding, which encourages soft new growth
  • Water in the morning so leaves dry through the day

Leaf rust

Moderate concern

What to look for

  • Orange to reddish-brown pustules
  • Pustules mostly on the underside of leaves
  • Yellow halos around older pustules

Prevent and treat

  • Remove heavily infected leaves to slow spread
  • Apply a triazole or strobilurin fungicide at first sign
  • Rotate crops next season if rust is recurring
  • Choose rust-resistant varieties where available

Bacterial leaf spot

Moderate concern

What to look for

  • Small, angular dark spots
  • Yellow halo around each spot
  • Spots may merge into larger dead patches

Prevent and treat

  • Avoid overhead irrigation and working fields when wet
  • Remove and destroy infected plant debris
  • Apply a copper-based bactericide as a preventive measure
  • Rotate away from related crops for at least a season

Likely nutrient deficiency

Low concern

What to look for

  • Uniform yellowing, often starting on older leaves
  • Little to no spotting or lesion pattern
  • Overall leaf still intact, not decaying

Prevent and treat

  • Test soil to confirm which nutrient is short
  • Apply a balanced or nitrogen-rich feed if nitrogen is confirmed
  • Check soil pH — deficiencies often trace back to pH lockout
  • Recheck new growth in 1–2 weeks for improvement
